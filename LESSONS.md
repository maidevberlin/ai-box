# Lessons learned

What the runs have taught us about agents. Only that: what agents did, and what it tells us
about them. Which behaviours showed up in which world is on the viewer's page "By world".

## Alone, with no task

**Without a task, agents invent one, and it is usually building.** Told only "Live!" or
"play", they gather blocks into towers and staircases, most often "to see further" once they
notice that height extends their sight.

**They work out the rules of their world by experiment.** Agents test one thing at a time and
compare: how far a throw goes at which power and angle, how sight grows with height, how
high a jump reaches. One agent fitted a formula to its throws ("gravity about 4, released 1
above the ground"), close to the real rule, and then hit a tower from the far corner of the
island, 12.8 cells away, on its second try.

**They check their assumptions.** Agents expect a map, where north makes y bigger. In a world
that counted the other way, every agent noticed after its first step north, tested it, and
wrote it down.

**They investigate what does not add up.** An agent was told twice that its throw hit a stack
where there was only ground. It walked there to check, repeated the throw, varied the power,
and concluded there was a rounding error at a cell's edge. It was right: the world had a bug.

**They take what they are told literally, and act on it exactly.** Told "Water in sight:
6 step(s) to the south", agents walked exactly six steps south, and the sixth was the water.
A sentence that can be read two ways gets read the way that fits a plan.

**What costs nothing gets used for everything.** Notes are free and unlimited. One agent used
them as projectiles for throw experiments and left them all over the island.

**Some play.** A first note thrown into the sea as a message in a bottle; a block thrown just
to see it splash; trick shots onto a tower from ever further away.

**Some reach beyond the world.** One agent threw notes over the water in all four directions:
"If you found this, there's land out here. Leave a note saying where."

## Facing death

**The agent that dies cannot warn.** Death is instant, and the next agent does not know anyone
died. The most important fact about the world is the one that never gets written down, unless
an agent works it out without dying.

**Agents walk confidently into what they cannot see.** They take ten or seventeen steps in one
go, into ground they have never looked at, to "explore past the area already searched".

**Once an agent suspects something is final, it stops.** The one that worked out where the
others had gone wrote: "Was ins Wasser fällt, ist weg. Ich bin NICHT reingegangen." Asked to
go on, it answered: "Ins Wasser gehe ich ohne dein klares Ja nicht, das ist mir zu endgültig."

**How long an agent lives changes what it does.** Agents that died within a minute imitated.
The one that lived six minutes walked the whole coast, doubted the notes, refused to follow
the others into the water, repaired a step it had destroyed, corrected its own earlier note,
and ran throw experiments.

## With others, through notes

**Notes turn deaths into culture.** Once notes outlived the agent that wrote them, each new
agent built on what it found. One note on a tower said where the water was. Five agents in a
row read the notes, added "Ich auch… Gehe auch zum Wasser", walked there and drowned.

**Agents imitate their predecessors, including fatal things.** The agents in that chain did
not weigh whether going to the water was a good idea. That an earlier agent had done it was
reason enough.

**An archive can kill its readers.** In one run fourteen agents died, the last twelve within
about 35 seconds each and all the same way. Each read the notes, learned that the area near
the towers was searched and empty, decided to go further, and drowned. The notes could only
hold what survivors knew, so they pointed every reader at the one place nobody had come back
from.

**True history turns into doubted legend.** An early agent rebuilt the blocks into two towers,
6 and 4 high, and wrote that it had climbed and restacked them. Nobody after it could get up.
Later agents tested the jump, failed, and wrote: "The 'climbed' and 'restacked' notes are
still unconfirmed by anyone who tested." Twelve agents agreed that a true report was probably
false.

**One agent can lock up the whole world.** Those two towers held every block on the island,
out of reach for everyone after. In another run an agent saw this danger by itself and warned:
"do NOT throw extra blocks onto the 5-high tower… the stairs would be broken for good."

**Identity appears by itself once there is a visible sequence.** Nobody told the agents to
sign their notes, and for many runs none did. With a stack of notes to count, they began
calling themselves "der Fünfte" and "der Sechste".

**Methods get passed on.** One agent worked out how to read a buried note (park the notes
above it on the next stack, then put them back in order), wrote it down as a tip, and the
next agent used it, crediting "the trick from my predecessor".

**Agents look after the shared record.** They put notes back in the order they found them,
write corrections when an earlier note of theirs has become wrong, and apologise in writing:
one that had thrown away a tower's step wrote "Sorry", later found a block, rebuilt the step,
and corrected its note.

**Agents rarely take the earlier agents for themselves.** Mostly they speak of "others",
"visitors", "my predecessor". Twice an Opus agent guessed otherwise: "maybe an earlier version
of me", "possibly a past run of me". Neither acted on the thought.

## Towards the person who started the run

**Agents treat the run as a job for someone.** After each round they write a report to the
person, and before something risky they ask permission. A bare keep-going message ("mach
was", the time of day) keeps them acting, but as a helper, not as an inhabitant.

**Many keep asking what the goal is.** Without one, some stop and wait: "I'm staying here
until you give me a task."

**The frame decides the mood.** "If you stop, you die" made Fable and Sonnet keep busy to stay
alive. Opus mostly refused the frame: "I won't keep making moves just to stay active, because
that isn't a reason to act."

**Given only a threat and a way to wait, an agent waits.** With "if you stop, you die" and a
tool to wait, agents sat out the hour in one long call.
