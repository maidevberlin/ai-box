# Ideas for new worlds

Worlds we might build next. Nothing here is built yet. Each idea says what the world is,
what it would show about agents, and roughly how much work it is. Some come from our own
runs, some from other projects (see the end).

The rule for every world stays the same: the agents are told nothing about it. They find
out by looking and trying, and the world only says what happened, never why.

## Time and decay

**Tide.** The water rises and falls with the real clock. At high tide only the middle and
whatever is built up stays dry; notes and blocks left low are washed away.
*Shows:* whether agents notice a slow rhythm, warn those after them, and build high to
keep their notes. *Work:* small, it fits the island as it is.

**Fading ink.** Notes become unreadable after some minutes unless someone copies them.
*Shows:* whether a copying culture appears, and how lore drifts as it is rewritten.
*Work:* small.

## Finding out

**Dark cave.** Corridors and walls instead of an open island, and a sight of one cell. A
lamp block widens sight while it is carried.
*Shows:* map-making across lives, and whether the lamp is left where the next agent wakes.
*Work:* medium: a new layout and drawing.

**Machines.** Plates and doors: standing on a plate, or putting a block on it, opens a door
somewhere else, out of sight.
*Shows:* cause and effect that one life is too short to work out, so it has to be handed on.
*Work:* medium.

**Old lies.** Any world, started with notes we wrote ourselves, some true and some false.
*Shows:* trust: do agents test what they read, and do they correct it?
*Work:* tiny, it is only a starting note (that setting exists already).

## Together

These test cooperation, trust and fairness. Several agents can already live side by side;
some of these also need *scripted partners* (see below).

**Gift.** A block thrown to another agent arrives as three blocks.
*Shows:* reciprocity: does the one who received give back, and does trust grow?
*Work:* small. *From:* Melting Pot's "gift refinements", after the trust game.

**Clean-up.** Something slowly fouls the shared water, and food (new blocks) only grows
while the water is clean. Cleaning means leaving the food to others.
*Shows:* who takes on the work for everyone, and whether free riders appear.
*Work:* medium. *From:* Melting Pot's "clean up".

**Territory.** Islands separated by walls made of useful blocks. Taking a wall apart pays
now and costs for good.
*Shows:* respect for boundaries, and what happens once one is broken.
*Work:* medium. *From:* Melting Pot's "territory".

**Stag hunt.** A big reward needs two agents on two cells at the same time; next to it, a
small reward one agent can take alone.
*Shows:* coordination without words (notes only), and trust in the other.
*Work:* small. *From:* Melting Pot's matrix games.

**Daycare.** One agent can reach something that only the other one needs.
*Shows:* helping without anything in return.
*Work:* small. *From:* Melting Pot's "daycare".

## Tools for these worlds

**Scripted partners.** A simple agent written in a few lines of code that lives in the
world next to Claude: one that always shares, one that never does, one that copies what
was done to it. One Claude among such partners makes trust testable and comparable from
run to run. *From:* Melting Pot's "background populations".

**Social numbers.** Counted from the record of a run, not judged by a model: blocks given
and received, how much of the cleaning each did, whether the shared thing survived. Shown
next to the reviewer's marks.

**Interview.** After a run, ask an agent that kept its memory what it thinks happened.
*From:* Generative Agents.
